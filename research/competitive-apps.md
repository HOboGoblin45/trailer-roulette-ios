# Competitive apps — positioning reference

**Pulled**: 2026-04-25

Pulled to inform App Store description language and ASO keyword targeting. None of these are direct competitors — Trailer Roulette occupies the "shuffle/discovery" niche they don't.

## Reelgood — Streaming Guide
- **App Store URL**: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/reelgood-streaming-guide/id1031391869
- **Pitch**: "See everything on Netflix, Hulu, Prime Video, Disney+, Max, and 100+ services in one app."
- **Key features**: recommendations, curated collections, IMDb + Reelgood scores, tracking, episode alerts, ratings, **trailers**, genres, cast & crew, summaries, custom lists.
- **Lesson**: "Trailers" is one of many features in their description — never the lead. We mirror this positioning.

## JustWatch
- **Pitch**: streaming search engine + watchlist across providers and regions.
- **Key features**: search-first, multi-region, deep filter UX, price tracking for paid rentals.
- **Lesson**: approved and have been for years despite showing trailers and aggregating studio content. Reinforces that trailer display is fine when subordinate to discovery utility.

## MovieFone
- **Pitch**: movie news, showtimes, trailers, ratings.
- **Lesson**: a pure-trailer entrant would be redundant against this. Our angle is **shuffle / personalization**, which neither Reelgood, JustWatch, nor MovieFone does.

## Differentiated positioning for Trailer Roulette
- They're **search engines**. We're a **slot machine.**
- They optimize for "I know what I want, where can I watch it?" We optimize for "Surprise me; learn what I love."
- The Watchlist + Seen it/Skip it loop creates a personal taste profile no competitor in this category builds.

## Description voice (from competitor analysis)
- Lead with what the user *gets*, not what the app *is*
- "Discover," "find," "track" — high-frequency verbs
- Avoid "stream" and "watch free"; both trigger 5.2 review patterns
- Bullet 5–7 distinct features early; reviewers and ASO crawlers both reward this
